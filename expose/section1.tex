\section{Problem Statement}
Image focus detection aims to distinguish focused (sharp) regions from
unfocused (blurred) regions within an image and is widely used in image
analysis and quality assessment. A common strategy is to segment images into
regions and classify these regions according to their focus characteristics.

Traditional machine learning approaches for this task rely on handcrafted
feature extraction followed by supervised classification. Although such methods
are computationally efficient, their robustness is often limited by the
expressiveness of manually designed features, and they may suffer from
overfitting when image conditions vary. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs),
in contrast, are able to automatically learn discriminative features and
generally exhibit stronger robustness. However, this capability typically comes
at the cost of increased data requirements, making CNN-based methods less
effective in scenarios with limited training samples or strict efficiency
constraints.

As a result, it remains unclear whether traditional machine learning methods or
lightweight CNN architectures provide a better trade-off between robustness,
accuracy, and data efficiency for segmentation-based image focus detection.
This research addresses this question through a systematic comparative study of
both approaches.
